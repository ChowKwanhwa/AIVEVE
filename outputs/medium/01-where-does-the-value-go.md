Sometime in early 2024, the cost of generating a high-quality AI image dropped below a tenth of a cent. Two years later, in the early months of 2026, it's flirting with prices that round to zero. The model layer of generative AI has commoditized in record time — historically fast, even by software standards.

The technology has democratized.

The economics has not.

Walk into any of the dominant AI consumer products today and you will find a payment model invented in 2023, on the SaaS playbook from 2018: a monthly subscription, billed quarterly, settling somewhere on a centralized balance sheet. The user pays. The platform collects. The surplus produced by your creativity flows into a small set of corporate treasuries and accrues to no one else.

This is the AI consumer paradox in 2026: the technology is open, the cost has collapsed, and yet the value capture model is a 2023 SaaS spreadsheet with a generative AI sticker on the front.

For people who came to crypto because they cared about value alignment, this is uncomfortable. For people who came to AI because they thought it would democratize creation, this is more uncomfortable still.

We started Aivive because we couldn't stop asking the same question.

> **Where does the value go?**

---

## Three patterns that don't answer

The default in AI consumer products today is one of three patterns. None of them answer the value-flow question.

**Pattern 1 — Subscription SaaS.** The Netflix model of AI. Predictable revenue for the platform, durable margin, opaque value capture. The user has no claim on what they helped produce; the platform has no obligation to surface where the surplus goes. This is the default, and it is durable, and it is also vulnerable — vulnerable to the first competitor that finds a more aligned shape.

**Pattern 2 — Token-as-fee.** The crypto mirror image. Make the user pay in your platform's native token. In theory, this aligns the user economically with the protocol. In practice, it adds onboarding friction, forces every user to think about price action, and frequently results in a token whose only utility is "discount on subscription" — economically indistinguishable from a coupon, with worse UX.

**Pattern 3 — Token-as-governance.** Issue a token. Promise governance rights. Hope the market values control over a small consumer app. Most of these tokens trade on speculation rather than fundamentals, because governance, in the absence of a real value sink, is not a price floor. It's a story.

None of these answer the actual question. None of them give the user — the person whose creativity is producing the surplus — a measurable, verifiable claim on the value flowing through.

---

## A different shape

We started thinking about a different shape in early 2025. The premise was simple, almost embarrassingly so:

> *What if the way you fund the product **were** the way the underlying network captures value?*

Not as a marketing claim. As a deterministic on-chain function.

What if every dollar of revenue the platform earned were, by design, converted into a measurable reduction of the underlying asset's supply? What if the loop ran on a public schedule, with every step verifiable by anyone, automatically, without human discretion? What if the user never had to hold the token to use the product — but every act of creation nonetheless contributed to the asset's compounding scarcity?

This isn't a thought experiment. The mechanics are well-understood. The buyback-and-burn pattern has been refined for years across crypto — BNB pioneered it at scale, GMX adapted it for derivatives, Jupiter has been iterating on it on Solana. None of them, until now, has applied it cleanly to the new wave of AI consumer applications.

The math is simple.

The infrastructure is mature.

The implementation is harder than it looks.

And the politics — the discipline required to actually run the loop without exception, in public, on a schedule, while resisting the thousand small temptations to *just make an exception this one time* — is harder still.

That last part is what we've been working on.

---

## Why this matters now

We don't think the consumer AI landscape of 2027 will look like 2024.

In 2024, it was acceptable to ship an AI tool with a chatbox and a Stripe subscription button and call it a product. The marginal cost of generation was high enough that the subscription was justified by inference cost alone. The tooling stack didn't yet support the alternatives.

In 2027, neither of those things will be true.

Inference will be effectively free. The tooling stack will be entirely capable of routing real economic value through verifiable on-chain primitives. And the users — particularly the next 100 million AI consumers, who are arriving from a default of *"I expect to participate in the economic upside of the platforms I make valuable"* — will not accept the SaaS shape forever.

The opening is real. The window is small. And the projects that ship the right answer first will define the category that everyone else gets compared to.

We're trying to be that project.
