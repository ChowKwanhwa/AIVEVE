In the [first essay](#)¹, we asked where the value goes in AI consumer products. The short answer is: nowhere a token holder can verify.

The longer answer requires a category that doesn't quite exist yet — at least not under a name. So we gave it one.

We call it a **Recursive AI Protocol**, or **RAP** for short.

A Recursive AI Protocol is an on-chain economic system with a single defining property: **each act of using the AI product directly compresses the supply of the protocol's underlying asset.** Not as a marketing claim. Not as a discretionary buyback program. As a deterministic on-chain function, executed on a public schedule, by code that runs whether anyone is watching or not.

The word that does the work here is *recursive*. It's worth slowing down on.

---

## What "recursive" means

In computer science, a recursive function is one that calls itself. The output of one iteration becomes the input of the next, until some condition terminates the loop. The power of recursion isn't in any single iteration — it's in the way each turn reinforces the next, often producing emergent behavior that's hard to predict from the first pass alone.

In the context of an AI consumer protocol, the recursion looks like this:

> **Use → Revenue → Supply ↓ → Value ↑ → More Use**

The user uses the product. The product generates revenue. The revenue is converted, mechanically and verifiably, into a reduction of the protocol's underlying asset supply. The reduction in supply, all else equal, increases the per-unit value of the asset. That increase in per-unit value attracts new users, who use the product, generating more revenue, compressing supply further.

Each turn of the loop reinforces the next.

This is what we mean by recursive.

It is also why we think this isn't just a different fee model. It's a different *category*.

---

## Why this is a category, not a feature

There are projects with one or two pieces of the recursion. Some have a buyback program but not a public schedule. Some have a public schedule but discretion in execution. Some have execution discipline but no consumer-facing surface to drive volume.

A Recursive AI Protocol is the configuration where all of these things hold simultaneously, by design:

- The consumer surface is real and used (the product has actual revenue).
- The conversion from revenue to supply reduction is automated (no human in the path).
- The schedule is public (no discretion to delay).
- The mechanism is end-to-end on-chain (no "trust us, we burned them last quarter").

When all four hold at once, the protocol becomes self-reinforcing in a way that is structurally different from any one of the four properties on its own.

This is what makes it a category. And categories, in our experience, get named once and remembered for a long time.

---

## Why now

You can ask: why didn't anyone build this in 2022 or 2023?

Two answers.

**First, the consumer surface wasn't ready.** AI generation was still expensive enough that the marginal cost of a "use" was a meaningful fraction of the revenue from that use. There wasn't enough surplus to convert into anything meaningful. The economics broke at small scale.

**Second, the on-chain infrastructure wasn't ready.** Cross-chain primitives that could reliably move stablecoin from one chain to another, on automated schedule, with verifiable burn-and-mint mechanics, simply didn't exist as production-grade systems until the last 18 months. The plumbing didn't work.

Both constraints have lifted. The model layer is functionally free. The on-chain plumbing is production-ready.

The window opened in 2025. We think it closes in 2027, once a few protocols have planted flags.

## What we're not

We are not a buyback program with extra steps.

We are not a community fund that quietly accumulates assets and occasionally announces a burn for marketing reasons.

We are not a governance experiment dressed up as an economic system.

We are a Recursive AI Protocol. The recursion is the point. The mechanism is just the way the recursion is implemented.

## What this changes

Once the recursive shape is real and running, three things change.

**The first thing that changes** is what a token holder is actually buying. In a typical token economy, holding the token is a bet on future utility — usually utility that doesn't exist yet, sometimes utility that won't exist for years. In an RAP, holding the token is a bet on the cultural relevance of the product running on top. If the product gets used more, the token gets rarer. The bet collapses to a single observable variable.

**The second thing that changes** is what a user is actually doing. In the Web2 default, the user is a customer paying for a service. In an RAP, the user is — without needing to think about it, hold anything, or sign anything beyond the product transaction itself — a participant in monetary policy. Every act of creation has a downstream effect on supply.

**The third thing that changes** is what the protocol team is actually being held accountable for. Not roadmap. Not marketing. Not the next listing. The team is held accountable for one thing: keeping the loop running. Public schedule, no exceptions, no discretion. That's the job.
